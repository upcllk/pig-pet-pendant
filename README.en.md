# Pig Pet Pendant

A small desktop pig pendant that follows your mouse. The macOS version is available; a Windows version is planned but not yet available.

![Preview of six modes](docs/media/modes.gif)

*The preview shows all six modes in sequence. The images were rendered by the app and combined automatically; they are not a screen recording.*

## Modes

- **Rope Pendant**: The default mode. The pig reacts to movement and falling, with randomly spaced blinks, ear movements, and body gestures at rest. Actions return smoothly to rest instead of looping the entire GIF.
- **Pig Balloons**: Shows 1–8 pig-shaped balloons floating upward; the default is 2. Each has independently timed blinks, gentle ear movements and nose gestures, with movement reactions.
- **Pig in a Net**: A pig in a net with a looping animation.
- **Pig on Bamboo**: The pig hugs a bamboo stalk that bends with mouse movement, with random blinks, ear movements and nose gestures that preserve its grip.
- **Hanging Pig**: Hangs from a rope with randomly timed ear twitches and nose gestures, reacting to movement and falling. Cross eyes do not blink; the purple band, attachment, and soft closed halo remain unchanged.
- **Ghost Pig**: Follows the mouse smoothly without a rope.

## Music Reactive Experiment

At launch, the app detects drum onsets from changes in low- and mid-frequency energy in system playback audio. The menu-bar icon grows on detected hits and falls back between them; the desktop pendant is unaffected. This is a real-time onset estimate, not full-song beat-grid or music recognition. On macOS 14.2 or later, first launch requests **System Audio Recording** permission solely to analyze playback locally in real time for drum onsets that drive the menu-bar icon; audio is not recorded or saved, and **Screen Recording** permission is not requested. The current permission or capture status is available in the status-item menu.

## Usage

Click the pig icon in the menu bar to switch modes or open **Settings**. Settings are saved automatically. Closing the Settings window does not quit the app; select **Quit Pig Pet Pendant** to exit.

## Build

Requires macOS 12 or later and Xcode Command Line Tools. From the repository root, run:

```bash
./build.sh
open dist/PigPendant.app
```

The app bundle and a ZIP archive for the current architecture are created in `dist/`. The app uses an ad hoc signature and is not notarized by Apple. When opened on another Mac for the first time, macOS may show a security warning.

To run the build, physics, and GUI smoke checks:

```bash
./build.sh --test
```

GUI checks require a logged-in desktop session. The test pendant appears briefly and exits automatically.

The transparent artwork `PigPendant/Resources/rope-pig.png` preserves all 75 original GIF frames and their 40 ms durations. Only the upper rope and distant stray pixels are removed; frames share an attachment point without redrawing the character. The original static `pig.png` is unchanged. Playback selects short, state-driven actions and blends similar poses instead of jumping across the original loop seam. Random gestures remain available after physics settles; waiting uses a lower update rate, and hiding or sleeping pauses playback. Re-extract with `swift tools/extract-rope-pig.swift source.gif PigPendant/Resources/rope-pig.png`. The [animation notes](docs/ROPE_ANIMATION.md) preserve the complete pose atlas, seam analysis, action mapping, and reproduction commands.

Balloon and bamboo characters share the action scheduler, not the rope pig's frames. Local pixel warps around their original eyes, ears and noses produce small gestures without redrawing the character, changing source files or stretching the whole pig. Balloon attachment pixels and bamboo foot pixels / grip geometry remain unchanged; these are not the GIF's full limb and body gestures. Balloons have independent idle timing; movement and actual downward motion trigger reactions, with smooth returns to rest. Character playback continues after physics settles and pauses while hidden or asleep. Working images are at most 512 pixels wide, with bounded pose caches. `./build.sh --test` checks eye shape, protected nose and foot pixels, all balloons' independent states, and pause/resume; it exports light-background pose atlases and scene sequences to `dist/`. These are automated renders, not screen recordings or confirmation of subjective naturalness.

Local gestures use 72 phase intervals and update at 60 Hz during playback; this is neither 72 FPS nor additional source artwork frames. Blinks progressively close, hold for about 0.2 seconds, reopen more slowly, and return to rest. Ears pivot around their roots, and nose gestures have a more visible local displacement. GUI checks also measure eye-height progression and ear/nose displacement at 48 pt with 2x pixels, rather than relying on enlarged previews. Original-frame rope animation is unaffected.

Hanging Pig maps blink actions to ear twitches, preserving its cross eyes. Ear/nose warps use its own source coordinates without modifying the original image; body motion remains driven by existing rope physics, and the halo has no independent animation. Local gestures continue at physical rest and pause while hidden or asleep. GUI checks measure normal-size feature displacement and protect cross eyes, the band, halo, and bottom attachment pixels. The light-background atlas is exported to `dist/hanging-local-poses.png` (automated rendering, not a recording).

## Known Limitations

- A Windows executable is not currently available.
- Balloons do not collide with each other and are not constrained by screen boundaries.
- macOS has no supported API for reading another app's global cursor visibility, so the pendant may remain visible over fullscreen video after the cursor is hidden.
- The app does not request Accessibility or Screen Recording permissions and does not send network requests.
